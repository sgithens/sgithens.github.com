## 2010-10-26 Tuesday, October 26th

This week we get to celebrate everyone’s favorite pagan holiday—Halloween. Halloween has its origins in the ancient Celtic festival called Samhain, literally “Summer’s End”. Samhain marked the end of the harvest season and the beginning of the descent into Winter—a transitional time, or cusp, between the forces of light and darkness. The belief was that the sharp line of demarcation between this world and the spirit world becomes more nebulous at this time and there is more interaction and communication between the two realms—spirits are roaming the earth and the veils between past, present and future may be lifted in prophecy and divination.

The Samhain Festival, from October 31st until November 2nd, was considered a time of “no time”, when structures were abolished and chaos would reign. People did crazy things—men dressed as women and women dressed as men, farmers’ gates were unhinged and left in ditches, peoples’ horses were moved secretly to different fields and children would knock on neighbors’ doors demanding food and treats (sound familiar?). Beyond the apparent lunacy was a deeper meaning. The Celtic priests, the Druids, knew that those days had a special quality to them. The veil between this world and the world of the ancestors was drawn aside, and, for those who were prepared, journeys could be made safely to the “other side” to contact the spirits of the departed as sources of inspiration and guidance.

This was also the Celtic New Year, celebrated with bonfires and feasting. One of the traditions was to make a list of one’s negative qualities and cast it into the fire as a gesture of purification and renewal.

The Samhain Festival occurs during the Sun’s annual transit of Scorpio from October 23rd until November 22nd. One of the symbols for Scorpio is the Phoenix, the mythical bird that is consumed by the solar fire and rises again out of its own ashes.

This week holds the potential for transformation and regeneration as long as we are willing to enter the fire of “tapas” and loosen our grip a bit on the rigidity of our personality structure, our social role and even our sense of the solidity of the world around us. It is the time to be more porous, spontaneous and receptive to influence from “the other side”.

Happy Halloween!

## 2010-10-19 Tuesday, October 19th

I.33  Maitri karuna mudita upeksanam sukha dukha punya apunya vishayanam bhavanatah citta prasadanam

“By cultivating loving kindness towards those who are happy, compassion for those who suffer, joy for the virtuous, and equanimity for the non virtuous we enter the blessed state of mind.”

In this beautiful sutra Patanjali offers a prescription to help us get our lives moving in a positive direction and overcome some of the negative feelings human beings are prone to—envy, callousness, inferiority, and intolerance.

Yogic philosophy, from the Upanishads, states that we all are a part of Brahman, “The Vastness”—essentially we are all “One”.  Unfortunately, it is all too easy to become disconnected to the “Oneness” and succumb to self-obsession, isolation, and petty head trips that create dysfunction and suffering.

To lift ourselves up, Patanjali offers four great altruistic qualities—loving kindness, compassion, joy, and equanimity.  Practicing these qualities will take us back towards the “Oneness” because they are a clear positive expression of our interconnectedness. This is the Law of Karma in action.

This week we are blessed with three lovely planetary trines—Mercury trine Neptune, Sun trine Neptune, and Mars trine Jupiter. These are all spiritualizing aspects, stimulating the desire for a deeper level of experience and connection, both intellectually and emotionally, and the aspiration to be of service to others. Neptunian aspects help us to expand the sense of self, to embrace the collective unconscious, and experience the Brahman, out of which we all arise.

As an example of compassion in action, the extraordinary Sequoia Neptune will be doing a benefit Kirtan Saturday October 23 at 7pm as a fundraiser to benefit our dear brother Rich McGowan.

As many of you know, Rich has been experiencing some serious health challenges during the past year which have been a drain on his financial resources. ## 2010-09-21 Tuesday, September 21st

On Wednesday September 22 at 8:09pm PDT, the sun passes over the equator on its way south. In the northern hemisphere this marks the Autumnal Equinox, a day when all over the planet we have equal parts of light and darkness. The Sun enters the sign of Libra represented by the scales of balance. The Equinox—Latin for “equal night”—is a day of perfect balance in the external world and is a potent symbol for the balance we seek in yoga.

The practice of Hatha Yoga (literally, the union of the Sun and the Moon) is the attempt to balance opposing but complimentary forces. In the Yoga Sutras Patanjali says, “Sthira sukham asanam,”—the essence of asana is stability and ease. He goes on to say, “Prayatna saitilyam ananta samapattibhyam”—this can be achieved through awareness of the interplay of effort and surrender. Once we have achieved this marriage of opposing but complimentary forces, “Tatah dvandva anabhighatah”—then one is no longer disturbed by duality.

When we practice asanas we have a very tangible experience of duality—the inhale and the exhale, the prana and the apana, the upward dog and downward dog, the right side and left side, the forward bend and backward bend, flexion and extension, etc. In each moment of practice we attempt to be stable yet relaxed, to exert appropriate effort and find appropriate surrender. It’s not an easy task, especially when faced with particularly challenging asanas. We can think of the asanas as metaphors for the various situations we encounter in life: learning to be firm without being stiff; to be soft and receptive without being a wimp, etc. Basically we are learning to apply our awareness to the task at hand, so we can adapt gracefully to changing circumstances. Another great duality presented by Patanjali is Purusa and Prakriti. Prakriti is the world of unfolding manifestation which is in a continual state of flux through the interplay of the Gunas: Sattva (equilibrium), Rajas (activity), and Tamas (inertia). Included in the realm of Prakriti is the body,  mind, and emotions. Purusa is the indwelling witness, the pure awareness that watches the dance of Prakriti. The Purusa is the ananta (the infinite) of which Patanjali speaks. The Sun symbolizes the Purusa, the eternally shining self. The Moon represents Prakriti, the ever changing world of Nature. Prakriti cannot see itself and Purusa is formless and motionless. Thus it is said, “Prakriti without Purusa is Blind and Purusa without Prakriti is Lame.”

Ultimately what we are seeking to discover in our asana practice is the changeless ground of being, the Purusa, as it delights in the dance of Prakriti. As the Sufis say, “What we are looking for is the thing that is doing the looking.

## 2010-09-14 Tuesday, September 14 2010

33 years ago when I first started practicing yoga, I began to notice that the same practice done on different days felt very different. The ease or difficulty of practice didn’t seem to necessarily have anything to do with the amount of sleep I had gotten, the purity of my diet, or the state of my love life. I was very curious about this phenomena and like any rational person, I began to investigate astrology. As an ashtanga practitioner, I was already tuned into to the lunar cycle so I wouldn’t mistakenly show up for class on a full or new moon. The waxing and waning the the moon explained some of this fluctuation of energy, but obviously there was something else going on as well.

I purchased an astrological calendar which had a daily listing of all the planetary aspects. Then I had to familiarize myself with all the glyphs (symbols) for the different planets and aspects. I discovered that trines, sextiles and conjunctions were good aspects, and squares and oppositions were not so good. Venus and Jupiter were benefic planets, while Mars and Saturn were malefic ones. What I began to understand is that astrology is an attempt to decipher a symbolic language that is being communicated to us through planetary activity and interaction, and that the macrocosm (the universe) is reflected within the microcosm (the individual).  For example, each of the visible planets in the solar system corresponds to one of the chakras – Venus to the anahata chakra, for example.

Over the years this has become a hobby of mine, checking the daily planetary aspects, observing how I am feeling, noticing the size and quality of my yoga classes and sensing the general vibe of the world at large. I try to stay objective about it all and just notice the subtle and not so subtle energetic trends.

As the planets pass through the different constellations of the Zodiac, their influence shifts. In Vedic astrologythe planets are called “grahas” (graspers). They are thought to grab the energy of the constellation they are transiting and pull it into the Earth’s energy field. Once their energy enters the Earth’s energy field, then the planets can grab us as well.

In the coming week the big planetary aspect is Jupiter conjunct Uranus at 29 degrees Pisces on Saturday, September 18th—Yom Kippur—the Jewish day of Atonement. A conjunction in Vedic astrology is known as a “Yoga”. This particular conjunction combines the expansiveness and optimism of Jupiter with the Uranian desire for freedom and new ways of expression. It occurs in the spiritualizing sign of Pisces, giving us the urge to merge with the Ocean of Being. There is a real desire now to break free of the old paradigm of conditioned thought and behavior, coupled with faith, insight and compassion. The time is ripe for accelerating our own evolutionary process and doing some real good in the world, based on enlightened humanitarianism.

Jupiter and Uranus have been close together for the past four months and will continue to be for the next four, conjuncting again on January 4th, 2011. This is a large window of opportunity for us to make significant progress in our journey of Self-Realization.

## 2010-09-07 Tuesday, September 7th 2010

An interesting combination of energies presents itself in the coming week. Mercury continues its retrograde until Sunday September 12. The final few days of a Mercury retrograde can be the most challenging of all. Time seems to slow down just when we want it to speed up, difficulties with communication frustrate us, our cars and our computers malfunction, and our personal issues loom large on our psychological landscape. Perhaps we are feeling physically exhausted as well and have an overwhelming desire to take a long nap. This urge to retreat is natural at this time. Indeed, the key word during Mercury retrograde is “re “—this is the time to reflect, to refine, to reassess, to reorganize,  to reconnect, and to retrieve things from the past that have proved valuable.

On Wednesday September 8 we have a new moon in Virgo at 3:30am. This is the beginning of a new energy cycle, but Virgo is ruled by Mercury (which as we all know,  is retrograde), so our impulse towards starting new endeavors may be most productively channeled towards completion rather than creation,  towards inner work rather than outer work.

Thursday September 9 is Rosh Hashanah—Jewish New Year.  The next ten days in the Jewish faith are traditionally a time for inner work--reflection,  prayer, and repentance—leading up to Yom Kippur, the Day of Atonement on September 18.  This is a wonderful opportunity to make a realistic assessment of ourselves, to acknowledge where we have fallen short of our ideals, and to forgive ourselves and others for our sins of commission or omission so we can move forward in our lives with a greater sense of perspective and compassion.

As always,  it is a wonderful time to practice yoga and to remember the three critical elements for a successful sadhana—tapah, svadhyaya, and Isvara pranidhanani--purification, reflection, and devotion to God.  The Atonement of Yom Kippur and the At-One-Ment of Yoga are essentially the same thing—the removal of obstacles that prevent us from realizing our connection to the Divine.

## 2010-08-31 Tuesday August 31st 2010

As the sun makes it’s annual transit of Virgo until the autumnal equinox on September 22nd, we experience an increased sensitivity to matters relating to personal health. Mercury in retrograde until September 12th predisposes us to re-experience old, potentially dysfunctional patterns of body, mind and communication. For the yogi this is a fertile time for practice. Some of our most persistent patterns (samskaras) may be in our face now, asking for recognition at the very least, and perhaps offering the opportunity for resolution and release.

There is a tendency to wallow during Mercury retrograde and to externalize responsibility for our suffering by blaming our spouse, our job, the economy, the planets etc. Our yoga practice gives us an opportunity to recognize that we alone are responsible for the quality of our life. By calmly observing the patterns of our physiology, and the nature of our thoughts and feelings, we begin to identify with the source of our awareness rather than the content of it. This gives us clarity, perspective, and compassion in our journey of self inquiry during this Mercury retrograde cycle.

-Tim Miller

## 2010-08-24 Tuesday August 24, 2010

Regarding Tuesdays, Pattabhi Jois would sometimes say, “Tuesday is a bad day.”  It is related to the planet Mars which is associated with conflict and accidents.  Guruji never gave out new poses, and never shaved on a Tuesday.  Mars is linked with the manipura chakra, the fire center that burns behind the navel, and our desire to assert ourselves in the world.  The symbol for Mars is identical to the symbol for the male of the species, a circle with an arrow emanating from it, indicating the desire to penetrate.  This desire to penetrate one’s world is healthy and innocent in itself, and only creates conflict when done in a clumsy or insensitive way.

In our yoga practice the manipura chakra is the great meeting place of prana and apana (the in breath and the out breath), which generates tapas, the sacred fire that purifies mind and body.  Tuesday is a day when we feel more fiery than usual and would be well advised to channel this Martian energy into our yoga practice to penetrate the density of our mind and body.  In India Tuesdays are associated with Lord Hanuman, the great monkey hero of the Ramayana.  Hanuman represents the ability to transform this primal Martian energy into “tejas”—radiance, strength, courage, and penetrating insight—with which he can serve Lord Rama with unwavering devotion.  The Hanuman Chalisa (forty verses to Hanuman) is traditionally sung on Tuesday to invoke his qualities of strength, intelligence, and devotion.

On Tuesday August 24 the full moon in Pisces invites us to dive into our emotional depths.  Mercury retrograde in Virgo until September 23rd provides a chance for reflection on old thought patterns, feelings, and behavior, and the opportunity to complete unfinished business.
